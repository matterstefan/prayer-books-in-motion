# Prayer Books in Motion for Action discussion

## What the prototype offers

Prayer Books in Motion explores printed prayer books as material artefacts through existing GW, ISTC and MEI data. The website contains 5,297 MEI copies in three selectable collections. Users can follow a copy, compare selected printing places and languages, inspect schematic connections or explore geographical concentrations through circles and a heatmap.

Website: https://matterstefan.github.io/prayer-books-in-motion/

## A short demonstration

Start with the GW core and a selected printing city. Move the timeline between early ownership and the modern period, noting that locations are carried forward between records. Open a connection and inspect an individual copy and its MEI source. Add liturgical or devotional editions and compare the change in coverage. Finally switch to the heatmap, explaining that smooth zones visualise concentration rather than documented presence at every intervening point.

## Questions for discussion

Which scholarly questions can be explored with present catalogue coverage? Where might cataloguing gaps explain apparent geographical differences? How could examples link the material history of prayer texts (WG1) with networks of circulation and collecting (WG2), and with the study of devotional images and experience (WG3)?

## Possible follow-up

A demonstration could be offered at the next WG1 online meeting and relevant WG2/WG3 forums, or at the Lisbon Midterm Conference. The documented reuse method could support an Action teaching session, a contribution to the planned strategy for databases and DH tools, or a future virtual exhibition. These are proposals for discussion, not confirmed activities or completed Action deliverables.

The prototype does not infer devotional use from ownership, claim complete routes or replace the source catalogues. Its chief contribution is a reusable exploratory approach and a concrete basis for cross-disciplinary discussion.
