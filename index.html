Project information

What this map shows

This experimental project reuses publicly accessible catalogue and provenance data from GW, ISTC and MEI. It creates no new catalogue records. Titles, institutional names and source wording are retained in their original languages.

The map includes 5,297 MEI copy records directly linked to the selected editions. Indirect bound-with relationships remain in the data tables; their provenance is not automatically transferred to other copies. MEI data retrieved: 9 October 2026, including reused earlier queries. Place review: 10 October 2026.

The core collection, “GW (Horae and selected editions)”, combines the GW Horae corpus with selected additional editions. Two optional collections add editions identified through MEI’s bibliographic fields: “liturgy” in subject, and “literature-devotional” in subject or keywords. Both additions exclude all editions already in the core collection. They may overlap with each other; each copy is displayed and counted only once. Selecting the liturgical addition alone therefore does not show all liturgical editions in the complete dataset.

Only copies represented in MEI can be displayed here. The map does not represent all surviving copies or the complete historical circulation of these editions. Place coordinates are supplemented from GeoNames (CC BY 4.0); a locality point is sufficient for this prototype.

In the heatmap, each copy contributes once at its last identifiable local place for the chosen year. Country and region records are excluded. The radius is set in kilometres: nearby places blend into zones that grow with the map when zooming in. The radius is approximate because of map projection. Colours indicate relative concentration, not absolute copy counts. Their reference uses all recorded local places of the selected copies, independent of the chosen year and map view; it changes only with the selection or radius. These smoothed zones do not establish additional locations between recorded places. Small dots provide exact counts and links to copies.

The last identifiable location is carried forward until the next record, including open date ranges such as “1868–”. This does not mean that the copy remained there throughout that period. The map includes later collecting and book-trade movements as well as early circulation.

Locations groups copies at the same map point for the selected year. Circle area is proportional to the number of distinct copies. Countries and regions use representative points with dashed outlines; these are not precise locations and are not stops on connection lines. Broad references to Europe or Western Europe have no map point.

Connections bundles shared links. Line width increases with the square root of the number of distinct copies and can be adjusted. Both directions share a line; individual directions appear in the connection panel. Hovering or clicking highlights only that connection. “Trace this copy” highlights the individual route.

Frequently recorded places act as cluster centres, weighted by distinct copies in the selection and year. Distance limits clustering, and zooming in separates places. These clusters are display aids, not historical territories. Links within a cluster are hidden; the copies remain accessible in the list.

Solid lines link adjacent, dated records with adjoining or overlapping periods and no explicitly flagged place uncertainty. Dashed lines indicate gaps, missing or conflicting dates, omitted records, or uncertain locations. Neither type establishes an actual travel route. The connection panel explains each link.

Reviewed coordinate variants share display points. Yale is shown at New Haven; Blickling Estate and Blickling Hall share a point. Original source data are retained. The disputed Fort-de-France reference in MEI 02128182 is displayed only as France, with the decision explained in the copy details.

Four historical or trade records have no known present holding location. Their last known locations remain marked as such. Undated or inconsistently dated stops are included in the full route view for 2026 but cannot reliably be assigned to earlier years. Unlocated binding and workshop records remain in the details.
