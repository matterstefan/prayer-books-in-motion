Project information

About Prayer Books in Motion

Prayer, print and material culture

Prayer Books in Motion is an experimental visualisation project developed by Stefan Matter in the context of PRAYTICIPATE — Participation through Prayer in the Late Medieval and Early Modern World (COST Action CA23143). It connects with Working Group 1’s interest in the material dimensions of prayer: printed prayer books are physical objects whose ownership, use and movement form part of the history of devotional culture.

The project asks what can be discovered by bringing together existing catalogue data and exploring them on a map. It is intended for researchers and other interested users as a starting point for further investigation, rather than a reconstruction of complete journeys or a comprehensive account of the circulation of prayer books. Suggestions and feedback from members of the Action have helped shape the prototype.

Reusing catalogue data

The project aggregates publicly accessible information from the Gesamtkatalog der Wiegendrucke (GW), the Incunabula Short Title Catalogue (ISTC) and Material Evidence in Incunabula (MEI). GW and ISTC identify editions; MEI supplies the individual copy records and their provenance information. Each displayed copy retains its MEI identifier and a link to the source record.

This is exclusively a data-reuse experiment: no new catalogue or provenance records are created. Data are linked, prepared for display and, where necessary, assigned reviewed geographical coordinates. These display decisions are documented separately from the source information. Catalogue titles, institutional names and other source wording remain in their original languages.

The three collections

The map contains 5,297 distinct MEI copy records across three selectable collections:

  • GW (Horae and selected editions): 531 copies. The core combines the GW Horae entries with selected additional editions relevant to printed prayer books.
  • Additional liturgical editions: 1,778 copies, identified through the term liturgy in MEI’s bibliographic subject field.
  • Additional devotional literature: 3,041 copies, identified through literature-devotional in MEI’s bibliographic subject or keyword fields.

Both additions exclude editions already in the core collection. They overlap with each other, but each copy is displayed and counted only once. Selecting the liturgical addition alone therefore does not show every liturgical edition in the dataset. These groupings follow catalogue entries and terminology; they do not propose a new definition of prayer books, liturgy or devotional literature.

Only copies with directly linked MEI records are displayed. Other copies recorded in GW or ISTC are not represented on the map. Indirect bound-with relationships remain in the underlying tables, but provenance is not automatically transferred between constituent prints. The visualisation follows individual copies of editions, rather than assuming that present-day bound volumes have always existed in the same form.

Reading the map and timeline

A copy’s sequence begins with its printing place where identifiable, followed by recorded provenance places and its present holding location where known. The timeline carries the last identifiable location forward until the next record, including open date ranges such as “1868–”. This does not establish continuous presence throughout the intervening period. Undated or inconsistently dated stations can appear in the full sequence for 2026 without being reliably assignable to earlier years.

Connections shows schematic links between recorded places. Solid lines indicate adjacent dated records with adjoining or overlapping periods and no explicitly flagged place uncertainty; dashed lines indicate gaps or uncertainty. Neither represents an established travel route. Wider connections represent more copies. The clustering control groups nearby places around frequently recorded locations; these are display aids, not historical territories. At a clustering value of 0, places remain separate. Connections inside a cluster are hidden, while the copies remain available in the list.

Locations — Circles groups copies at the same map point for the selected year; circle area represents the number of copies. Country and region references may use representative points, identified in their details, but are not local stops on connection lines.

Locations — Heatmap combines local points into softly overlapping zones using an approximate 200 km radius. Country and region references are excluded. The colours show relative concentration, not absolute copy counts or additional evidence between places. The colour reference uses all recorded local places of the selected copies and stays fixed across years and map views. Click a small point for the underlying copies.

Coverage and uncertainties

Patterns reflect both the surviving evidence and the uneven coverage of cataloguing. An absence of records is not evidence that a copy never visited a place. The map includes modern collecting and the antiquarian trade as well as earlier ownership. Four records without a known present holding location retain their last known location.

Coordinates describe localities rather than precise buildings. Ambiguous place names, broad geographical references and disputed assignments are handled conservatively. Original information is retained and reviewed decisions are documented, including the disputed Fort-de-France reference in MEI 02128182. Records without usable coordinates remain available in the copy details.

Digitisation links

Copy details distinguish links supplied for This copy in MEI from links for This edition in GW or ISTC. An edition-level link may show another copy; it does not establish an identity with the selected copy. Links may lead to complete scans, selected images or resource pages. Their completeness and continued availability have not been systematically verified. Provenance-image and watermark references are kept separate in the working data.

Sources, code and acknowledgements

The project depends on the work of the cataloguers and institutions contributing to GW, ISTC and MEI. Thanks are due to their teams for making these resources available, and to PRAYTICIPATE participants for their suggestions and feedback.

Locality coordinates are supplemented from GeoNames (CC BY 4.0). The map uses Leaflet and map data from OpenStreetMap contributors.

Code, data preparation and documented decisions are available in the Prayer Books in Motion GitHub repository. The website uses prepared data files; viewing the map does not trigger new catalogue queries. Catalogue retrieval can be repeated separately to prepare an updated dataset.
